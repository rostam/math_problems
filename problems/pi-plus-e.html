Problem 13 · Numbers themselves

Is π + e irrational?

We know π and e are each irrational, but not whether their sum or product is.

Statement. Is π + e irrational? Is πe? Is Euler's constant γ = lim (1 + 1/2 + ⋯ + 1/n − ln n) ≈ 0.5772 irrational?
π transcendental
Lindemann, 1882
e transcendental
Hermite, 1873
Known
At least one of π + e and πe is transcendental

Why it matters

These questions show how little we can prove about specific numbers. Almost every real number is transcendental, yet proving it for any particular number is extremely hard.

They test transcendence theory directly. A central conjecture there, Schanuel's conjecture, would settle π + e and thousands of similar questions at once.

Where things stand

Open. One easy fact: π + e and πe can't both be rational, or even both algebraic. If they were, π and e would be roots of x² − (π+e)x + πe, making them algebraic, which they are not. But we don't know which of the two is transcendental.

For γ, not even irrationality is known. If γ were a fraction, its denominator would need hundreds of thousands of digits.

Some related results are known. eπ is transcendental (Gelfond–Schneider). π and eπ are algebraically independent (Nesterenko, 1996). ζ(3) is irrational (Apéry, 1978).

History of attempts

  • 1737Euler proves e is irrational.
  • 1761Lambert proves π is irrational.
  • 1873–82Hermite (e) and Lindemann (π) prove transcendence.
  • 1934Gelfond and Schneider: ab is transcendental for algebraic a ≠ 0, 1 and irrational algebraic b. This gives eπ = (−1)−i.
  • 1960sSchanuel formulates his conjecture.
  • 1978Apéry shocks the community by proving ζ(3) is irrational.
  • 1996Nesterenko proves π, eπ and Γ(1/4) are algebraically independent.

Ideas for solving it

  • Schanuel's conjecture. It implies that π and e are algebraically independent, which settles the question immediately. Proving it would be a revolution.
  • Apéry-style approximations. Find a sequence of fractions converging to γ (or π + e) so fast that the number can't be rational. This is how irrationality of ζ(3) was proven.
  • Periods. Kontsevich and Zagier define "periods" (integrals of algebraic functions). π is a period, and e and γ are conjectured not to be. Understanding periods may clarify which relations can hold.

Try it yourself

Compute π + e ≈ 5.859874482… to many digits and find its continued fraction expansion (many calculators and websites do this). Rational numbers have finite continued fractions. Does yours look like it will stop?
