Problem 15 · Graph theory

Reconstruction Conjecture (Kelly–Ulam)

Suppose you are given every copy of a graph with one vertex deleted, as an unlabeled "deck of cards." Can you always rebuild the original graph, as long as it has at least 3 vertices?

Statement. Every graph with at least 3 vertices is determined, up to isomorphism, by its deck: the multiset of unlabeled graphs obtained by deleting each vertex in turn.
First posed
Kelly and Ulam, 1941–42
Verified
All graphs up to 13 vertices
Almost all graphs
Reconstructible from 3 cards

Why it matters

It asks how much global information is stored in local pieces. That is a central theme across mathematics and computer science.

Similar questions arise elsewhere: reconstructing molecules from fragments in chemistry, DNA from reads in biology, and structures from partial views in data science.

Where things stand

Open. Proven for many classes: trees, disconnected graphs, regular graphs, and maximal planar graphs. Bollobás showed that almost every graph is determined by just 3 of its cards.

The conjecture fails for directed graphs (Stockmeyer's infinite families), for hypergraphs and for infinite graphs. So any proof must use something special about finite undirected graphs.

A related edge reconstruction conjecture (using edge-deleted cards) is proven for graphs with many edges relative to the number of vertices (Lovász, Müller).

History of attempts

  • 1941Kelly and Ulam formulate the conjecture.
  • 1957Kelly proves it for trees. "Kelly's lemma": the deck determines how many copies of each smaller subgraph the graph contains.
  • 1972–77Lovász and Müller prove edge reconstruction for dense graphs.
  • 1977Stockmeyer shows reconstruction fails for directed graphs.
  • 1990Bollobás: almost every graph is reconstructible from 3 cards.
  • 1997McKay verifies all graphs up to 11 vertices, later extended to 13.

Ideas for solving it

  • Counting (Kelly's lemma). Extract ever more invariants from the deck, such as the degree sequence, spectrum, subgraph counts and the characteristic polynomial. The hope is to eventually pin down the whole graph.
  • Minimal counterexamples. Show a smallest counterexample must be highly structured (for example, nearly regular with few automorphisms) and then rule those out.
  • Algebraic and spectral methods. Recent work studies which spectral data the deck determines and uses it for special classes.

Try it yourself

Draw a 4-vertex path and a star with 3 leaves. Write out the deck of each (4 cards, each a 3-vertex graph). Can you tell them apart from the decks alone? Then try to find two different graphs with the same deck. (Only the 2-vertex graphs work, which is why the conjecture needs 3 or more vertices.)
