Problem 16 · Graph theory

Graceful Tree Conjecture

A tree is a connected graph with no loops. Take any tree with n vertices. Can you label its vertices 0, 1, …, n−1 so that the edge differences |a − b| are exactly 1, 2, …, n−1, each used once?

Statement. Every tree has a graceful labeling: its n vertices can be labeled with 0, 1, …, n−1 so that the n−1 edge labels |a − b| are all distinct.
First posed
Ringel–Kotzig, via Rosa, 1960s
Verified
All trees up to 35 vertices
Ringel's conjecture
Proven for large n (2020)

Why it matters

Graceful labelings were introduced by Rosa to attack Ringel's conjecture: the complete graph on 2n+1 vertices can be split into 2n+1 copies of any tree with n edges. A graceful labeling gives such a split immediately by "rotating" the tree.

Graph decompositions like this are used in the design of experiments, error-correcting codes and scheduling.

Where things stand

Open. The conjecture is proven for paths, stars, caterpillars (trees where every vertex is on or next to a central path), trees with at most 4 leaves, and many other families. It has been checked by computer for every tree with up to 35 vertices.

Ringel's conjecture itself was proven for all large n in 2020, independently by Montgomery–Pokrovskiy–Sudakov and by Keevash–Staden. They used probabilistic "absorption" methods instead of graceful labelings.

Montgomery and Pokrovskiy also showed that every tree has an almost graceful labeling, where all but a tiny fraction of edge labels are distinct.

History of attempts

  • 1963Ringel poses his decomposition conjecture.
  • 1967Rosa introduces "β-valuations" (later called graceful labelings). The conjecture that every tree is graceful becomes known as the Ringel–Kotzig conjecture.
  • 1970s–2000sHundreds of papers prove special cases. Gallian's dynamic survey lists thousands of results on graph labelings.
  • 2010Computer verification reaches all trees with up to 35 vertices.
  • 2020Ringel's conjecture proven for large n (Montgomery–Pokrovskiy–Sudakov; Keevash–Staden).

Ideas for solving it

  • Absorption and randomness. Label most of the tree randomly, keep a flexible reserve, and use it to fix the remaining conflicts. This works approximately. Making it exact is the challenge.
  • Transformations. Find operations that turn a graceful labeling of one tree into a graceful labeling of a modified tree, and connect every tree to a known graceful one.
  • Structural induction. Handle trees by their diameter, number of leaves or other structure, extending the long list of proven classes.

Try it yourself

Draw a star with 4 leaves and label the center 0 and the leaves 1, 2, 3, 4. That's graceful. Now draw a path with 5 vertices and try labeling it 0, 4, 1, 3, 2. Check the differences. Then pick a random tree with 7 vertices and find a graceful labeling by hand.
