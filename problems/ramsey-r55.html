Problem 14 · Graph theory

Ramsey number R(5,5)

How many people must be at a party to guarantee that 5 of them all know each other, or 5 of them are all strangers? For groups of 3 the answer is 6, and for groups of 4 it is 18.

Statement. Find the smallest N such that every red/blue coloring of the edges of the complete graph on N vertices contains 5 vertices whose connecting edges are all the same color.
Known range
43 ≤ R(5,5) ≤ 46
R(3,3), R(4,4)
6 and 18
Conjectured value
43

Why it matters

Ramsey theory is the mathematics of "complete disorder is impossible": any large enough structure contains an orderly piece. R(5,5) is the smallest diagonal case we can't compute, so it is a benchmark for both theory and computation.

Erdős's 1947 lower bound for Ramsey numbers launched the probabilistic method, now one of the most important tools in combinatorics and computer science.

Where things stand

Open. Exoo (1989) found a coloring on 42 vertices with no single-colored group of 5, so R(5,5) ≥ 43. In 2024, Angeltveit and McKay used massive computation to show R(5,5) ≤ 46. The widely believed answer is 43.

For large k, R(k,k) lies between roughly √2k and 4k. In 2023, Campos, Griffiths, Morris and Sahasrabudhe made the first exponential improvement to the upper bound in about 90 years, to below 3.993k.

History of attempts

  • 1930Frank Ramsey proves that Ramsey numbers exist.
  • 1935Erdős and Szekeres give the upper bound of about 4k.
  • 1947Erdős proves the lower bound √2k by showing a random coloring works. This is the birth of the probabilistic method.
  • 1955Greenwood and Gleason: R(3,3) = 6 and R(4,4) = 18.
  • 1989Exoo: R(5,5) ≥ 43.
  • 1995–2018Upper bound lowered step by step: 49, then 48.
  • 2023Campos–Griffiths–Morris–Sahasrabudhe: exponential improvement for R(k,k).
  • 2024Angeltveit and McKay: R(5,5) ≤ 46.

Ideas for solving it

  • Smarter exhaustive search. Use symmetry reduction, SAT solvers and linear programming to rule out 2-colorings of 45, 44 and eventually 43 vertices.
  • Flag algebras. Razborov's semidefinite-programming method gives computer-verified upper bounds for small Ramsey-type numbers.
  • Better constructions. A 43-vertex coloring with no monochromatic 5-clique would disprove the conjecture that the answer is 43. Searches so far have found none.

Try it yourself

Prove R(3,3) = 6. Take 6 people. Person A knows at least 3 of the others, or is a stranger to at least 3. Say A knows 3 people. If any two of them know each other, those two plus A are 3 mutual acquaintances. If not, those 3 are mutual strangers. Then find a 5-person coloring (hint: a pentagon and a pentagram) that avoids both patterns.
