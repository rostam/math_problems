Problem 5 · Number theory

Riemann Hypothesis

A claim about where the zeros of a certain function lie. Put simply, it says the primes are spread out as evenly as they can be.

Statement. Every non-trivial zero of the Riemann zeta function ζ(s) = 1 + 2−s + 3−s + ⋯ has real part exactly 1/2.
First posed
Bernhard Riemann, 1859
Prize
$1,000,000 (Clay Millennium Problem)
Zeros checked
more than 10¹³, all on the line

Why it matters

Riemann found an exact formula linking the primes to the zeros of ζ. Each zero contributes a "wave" to the count of primes. The hypothesis is equivalent to saying the number of primes up to x differs from the smooth estimate Li(x) by at most about √x · ln x. That is the smallest error one could hope for, so the primes would be as regular as possible.

Hundreds of theorems are proved "assuming RH" or its generalizations. One example: under the generalized RH, the Miller primality test is deterministic and fast. A proof would upgrade all of them at once.

It sits at a crossroads of number theory, analysis, random matrix theory and even quantum physics.

Where things stand

Open. Numerically, more than ten trillion zeros have been checked and all lie on the critical line. Theoretically, we know a positive proportion of zeros lie on the line: currently over 41%.

The analogue of RH for curves and varieties over finite fields is proven, by Weil and later Deligne, and it is one of the triumphs of 20th-century mathematics. Many hope a similar structure can be found for the integers.

History of attempts

  • 1859Riemann's only paper on number theory states the hypothesis.
  • 1896Hadamard and de la Vallée Poussin prove the prime number theorem by showing no zeros lie on the line Re(s) = 1.
  • 1914Hardy proves infinitely many zeros lie on the critical line.
  • 1942Selberg shows a positive proportion do.
  • 1948–74Weil (curves) and then Deligne (general varieties) prove the finite-field analogue.
  • 1973Montgomery and Dyson notice that spacings between zeros match eigenvalues of random matrices.
  • 1989Conrey: at least 40% of zeros are on the line.
  • 2018Rodgers and Tao prove the de Bruijn–Newman constant Λ ≥ 0. Since RH is equivalent to Λ ≤ 0, RH is true "only barely", if at all.
  • 2024Guth and Maynard improve Ingham's 1940 bound on zeros away from the line, the first major advance there in 80 years.

Ideas for solving it

  • Hilbert–Pólya. Find a natural self-adjoint operator whose eigenvalues are the zeros. Self-adjointness would force them onto the line. The random-matrix statistics strongly suggest such an operator exists.
  • Copy the finite-field proof. Deligne's proof uses cohomology and a Frobenius action. The dream is a "field with one element" or some other geometry in which the integers behave like a curve.
  • Zero-density and moment estimates. Incremental progress, such as Guth–Maynard, shows fewer zeros can stray from the line. This doesn't prove RH, but it gives many of its consequences unconditionally.
  • Positivity criteria. RH is equivalent to many positivity statements, for example Li's criterion and Weil's explicit formula. Proving one of them directly is another route.

Try it yourself

Compare π(x), the number of primes up to x, with Li(x) for x = 1,000, 10,000 and 100,000 using any online calculator. See how small the difference is compared with √x.
