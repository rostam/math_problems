Problem 21 · Graph theory

Cycle Double Cover Conjecture

Take any graph with no bridge, meaning no single edge whose removal disconnects it. Can you always pick a collection of cycles that covers every edge exactly twice?

Statement. Every bridgeless graph has a collection of cycles such that every edge lies in exactly two of them.
First posed
Szekeres (1973), Seymour (1979)
Minimal counterexample
Must be a snark
Known
Covers of every edge exactly 4 or 6 times

Why it matters

For planar graphs it is easy: the boundaries of the faces cover every edge twice. The conjecture says every bridgeless graph behaves as if it could be drawn on some surface with every face bounded by a cycle. That links it to surface embeddings.

It sits in a cluster of central conjectures about snarks and flows, including Tutte's 5-flow conjecture and the Berge–Fulkerson conjecture. Progress on one often informs the others.

Where things stand

Open. It is true for planar graphs, for cubic graphs with a 3-edge-coloring, and for graphs with a nowhere-zero 4-flow (Jaeger), among others.

A smallest counterexample must be a snark: a cubic graph that cannot be 3-edge-colored, like the Petersen graph. It is known that such a counterexample would need very large girth (shortest cycle length).

Weaker versions are proven. Every bridgeless graph has a family of even subgraphs covering each edge exactly 4 times (Bermond, Jackson and Jaeger, 1983) or exactly 6 times (Fan, 1992). "Exactly twice" remains open.

History of attempts

  • 1973Szekeres poses the problem for cubic graphs.
  • 1979Seymour poses it for all bridgeless graphs.
  • 1979Jaeger shows graphs with a nowhere-zero 4-flow have a cycle double cover.
  • 1983Bermond, Jackson and Jaeger: a 4-fold cover by 7 even subgraphs.
  • 1992Fan: a 6-fold cover by 10 even subgraphs.
  • 2000sMinimal counterexamples are shown to need large girth. Computers verify snarks up to large sizes.

Ideas for solving it

  • Study snarks. Since counterexamples must be snarks, understanding their structure is the main line of attack. Computer enumerations of snarks up to 36 or more vertices have found none that fail.
  • Flows. Strengthen flow results (for example, a proof of Tutte's 5-flow conjecture) and relate them to cycle covers.
  • Strong embeddings. Prove that every 2-connected graph embeds in some surface with every face a cycle. That would imply the conjecture.

Try it yourself

The Petersen graph has 15 edges, so a double cover needs total cycle length 30. Find six 5-cycles in the Petersen graph that together use every edge exactly twice. Then do the same for the cube using its 6 faces.
