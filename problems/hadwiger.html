Problem 17 · Graph theory

Hadwiger's Conjecture

A far-reaching generalization of the Four Color Theorem. Suppose a graph needs k colors so that neighboring vertices get different colors. Then, by deleting and contracting edges, you should be able to shrink it into k vertices that are all joined to each other.

Statement. Every graph with chromatic number at least k contains the complete graph Kk as a minor. Equivalently, graphs with no Kk minor can be colored with k−1 colors.
First posed
Hugo Hadwiger, 1943
Proven for
k ≤ 6
Best general bound
O(k log log k) colors

Why it matters

Bollobás called it "one of the deepest unsolved problems in graph theory." It explains why graphs need many colors: the only reason would be a large, tightly connected core.

The case k = 5 is equivalent to the Four Color Theorem, so the conjecture is a vast generalization of that famous result.

Where things stand

Proven for k ≤ 6. The case k = 6 (Robertson, Seymour and Thomas, 1993) reduces to the Four Color Theorem. Open for every k ≥ 7.

It is known that graphs without a Kk minor can be colored with O(k log log k) colors (Delcourt and Postle, 2021, building on Norin–Postle–Song). Proving even O(k), a linear bound, remains open.

It holds for almost all graphs (Bollobás, Catlin and Erdős, 1980). Hajós's stronger conjecture, which asks for subdivisions instead of minors, is false (Catlin, 1979).

History of attempts

  • 1937Wagner shows the k = 5 case is equivalent to the Four Color Conjecture.
  • 1943Hadwiger states the conjecture and proves it for k ≤ 4.
  • 1976Appel and Haken prove the Four Color Theorem, settling k = 5.
  • 1980Bollobás, Catlin and Erdős show it holds for almost all graphs.
  • 1984Kostochka and Thomason prove O(k √log k) colors suffice.
  • 1993Robertson, Seymour and Thomas prove k = 6.
  • 2019–21Norin, Postle and Song, then Delcourt and Postle, break the √log barrier: O(k log log k).

Ideas for solving it

  • Linear Hadwiger. Prove that Ck colors suffice for some constant C. This is the natural next milestone.
  • The case k = 7. Structure theory of graphs without a K₇ minor might settle it, but the analysis is expected to be enormous.
  • Graph minor theory. Robertson and Seymour's structure theorem describes graphs without a given minor. Turning that description into colorings is a long-term program.

Try it yourself

The complete graph K₄ needs 4 colors and is itself a K₄ minor. Now take an odd cycle of length 5 (it needs 3 colors) and find a K₃ minor by contracting edges. Can you find a planar graph that needs 4 colors? Its K₄ minor should be easy to spot.
