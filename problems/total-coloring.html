Problem 22 · Graph theory

Total Coloring Conjecture

Color both the vertices and the edges of a graph so that touching items always get different colors. If the busiest vertex has Δ edges, you clearly need at least Δ + 1 colors. Is Δ + 2 always enough?

Statement. Every graph with maximum degree Δ has a total coloring (of vertices and edges, where adjacent vertices, adjacent edges, and each vertex with its own edges all get different colors) using at most Δ + 2 colors.
First posed
Behzad and Vizing, mid-1960s
Proven for
Δ ≤ 5
Best general bound
Δ + C for a huge constant C

Why it matters

It unifies the two classical coloring problems. For edges alone, Vizing's theorem says Δ or Δ + 1 colors always suffice. The total coloring conjecture is the natural analogue when vertices and edges are colored together.

Colorings like this model scheduling problems where both resources (vertices) and interactions (edges) need time slots that don't clash.

Where things stand

Open. Proven for all graphs with Δ ≤ 5 (Rosenfeld and Vijayaditya for Δ = 3; Kostochka for Δ = 4 and 5). For planar graphs it is proven for every Δ except Δ = 6.

Molloy and Reed (1998) proved that Δ + C colors always suffice, for an absolute constant C. Their C was astronomically large (around 10²⁶), but it is a constant.

It is known exactly for complete graphs, complete bipartite graphs, cycles and many other families.

History of attempts

  • 1964Vizing proves edge colorings need only Δ or Δ + 1 colors.
  • 1965Behzad (and Vizing independently) conjectures Δ + 2 colors suffice for total coloring.
  • 1971Rosenfeld and Vijayaditya prove it for Δ = 3.
  • 1977–96Kostochka proves it for Δ = 4 and Δ = 5.
  • 1998Molloy and Reed: Δ + C colors suffice, using the probabilistic method.
  • 1990s–2000sPlanar graphs settled for Δ ≥ 7 using the discharging method.

Ideas for solving it

  • Probabilistic method. Refine the Molloy–Reed argument (random coloring plus the Lovász Local Lemma) to shrink the constant toward 2.
  • Discharging for planar graphs. The planar Δ = 6 case is the last one left, and fits the discharging technique used for the Four Color Theorem.
  • Reduce to edge coloring. Total coloring a graph is the same as vertex coloring its "total graph". Use structural results about those graphs, or embed the problem into an edge-coloring problem on a larger graph.

Try it yourself

Totally color a triangle: 3 vertices and 3 edges, with Δ = 2. You can do it with Δ + 1 = 3 colors (hint: give each edge the color of the vertex opposite it). Then try the complete graph K₄ (Δ = 3). Can you manage with 4 colors, or do you need Δ + 2 = 5?
